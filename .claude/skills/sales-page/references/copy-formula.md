# Copy Formula — Long-Form Direct-Response Sales Letter

Reverse-engineered from the "Claude Code Accelerator" workshop page. This is the
copy engine. Follow the **voice**, the **emphasis mechanics**, and the **section
sequence**. Quotes below are pulled from the source page so you can match cadence.

---

## 1. Voice & tone

- **First person, founder-to-friend.** One named person writes the whole letter
  ("A Shocking Message From [Name]"). It reads like a confession/story, not
  corporate marketing.
- **Raw and unpolished on purpose.** Contractions, profanity-adjacent punch
  ("a lazy fuck with smart systems", "remember that shit", "DAMN, THAT'S SEXY"),
  self-deprecation ("I'm basically a raccoon"). Tune the edge to the brand — keep
  the *energy* even if you drop the swearing.
- **Short paragraphs.** Most are ONE sentence. Many are 3–5 words. White space is
  a tool. Never write a 5-line paragraph.
- **Momentum via fragments.** "Not because we were lazy. Not because we 'wanted a
  break.' But because we decided to do something AMBITIOUS."
- **Conversational asides in parentheses.** Used constantly to add proof, humor,
  or a wink: "(Check the records. We've shifted towards creative strategy BEFORE
  it 'got hot.')"
- **Rhetorical questions** that pull the reader forward: "Think about that." /
  "Pretty cool, right?" / "So why tolerate it in your own workflow?"
- **Always "you"-focused on the payoff.** The story is about the writer; the
  *promise* is always about the reader.

## 2. Emphasis mechanics (the visual rhythm of the words)

- **ALL CAPS** on power/emotion words mid-sentence: `TRUE`, `SYSTEM`, `NEVER`,
  `REAL`, `FOR me`, `AROUND THE CLOCK`, `PARADIGM`. Use sparingly enough that it
  still hits — ~1–3 per paragraph.
- **Bold** for the load-bearing claim in a passage.
- **Section headers are bold CLAIMS, not labels.** Never "Our Curriculum."
  Instead: *"Within 48 Hours, Learning Claude Code Literally REMOVED The Limits Of
  What I Was Capable Of..."* Each header could stand alone as a tweet and advances
  the argument.
- **Big-number callouts** break the text wall: `50-100×`, `48 HRS`, `$25K`,
  `10× · 50× · 100×`. Each has a giant figure + a tiny caption underneath.
- **Numbered/laddered lists** for capabilities and credentials.

## 3. Section sequence (the format — top to bottom)

Use as many or as few as the offer needs, but keep this order.

1. **Pre-headline / personal frame** — `A Shocking Message From [Name]:`
2. **Story hook — the dramatic decision.** Open mid-action with a costly/risky
   choice. *"Two weeks ago, Luke and I made the decision to CANCEL all Genesis
   calls for the week."* Then the fragment triplet (Not… Not… But…).
3. **The build-up — what we set out to build.** Paint the scene with concrete,
   almost cinematic detail (the 6-bedroom Airbnb, 16-hour days, NDAs). Specifics
   = believability.
4. **Credibility stack.** A bulleted list of the "killers in the room," each line
   a hyper-specific credential ("A 20 year old media buyer who manages MILLIONS a
   month", "An AI consultant relied on by marketing legend Jay Abraham"). End with
   a humble/funny line ("Plus… some Luke guy. And myself.").
5. **The pivot / reveal.** Subvert expectation: *"But that's NOT what I'm here to
   talk about today… it's not just WHAT we built. It's also HOW we built it."*
   This is where you name the real subject.
6. **Personal transformation proof + timeline.** The "before I was a nobody at
   this" admission → the rapid result. *"When I Landed In Miami… I Had Never Even
   Opened A Claude Account!"* → *"By End Of Day Tuesday…"* Anchor with a number
   callout (`48 HRS · From Never Opening Claude → To Producing Cold-Traffic-Ready
   Ads`).
7. **Capability list — "what it can do FOR you."** Short stacked list: *Sales
   Copy · Static Images · Videos · Lead Magnets · Landing Pages · Apps · Software*
   then "Doesn't matter. ALL possible."
8. **Worldview / paradigm shift.** Reframe the reader's whole situation. *"This Is
   The NEW Way To Work."* Coin a sticky term ("getting CLAUDEPILLED"). Introduce
   stakes: *"in 2026, your WORK ETHIC no longer determines your OUTPUT."*
9. **Name the mechanism.** Give your method a proper-noun name: **"The Dual
   Flywheel System."** This is the single most important persuasion move — it
   turns a commodity ("AI training") into proprietary IP. Explain *why it
   matters* before *how it works*.
10. **Break the mechanism into parts.** *Flywheel #1: The System Gets Smarter* /
    *Flywheel #2: YOUR Judgment Gets Sharper.* Each part = a mini-argument with its
    own proof.
11. **Stakes / cost of inaction.** Twist the knife (kindly): *"If you're not
    building your OWN system, you aren't just falling behind. You're operating on
    BORROWED TIME."* The standards are rising; the gap widens daily.
12. **Offer introduction.** *"And on June 3rd, 4th, 5th… We're Hosting A Brand New
    LIVE VIP Workshop Called The [Offer Name]!"* State the promise simply right
    after: *"The promise of this workshop is SIMPLE: To take you from X… to Y."*
13. **Agenda / deliverables, day by day.** `Day 1 · [date]` badge, a CLAIM-style
    title, "Led by [Name]" with a credential-dense bio, then bullet *outcomes*
    (what the attendee will be able to DO), not topics. Repeat per day. Add a
    `BONUS · [date]` session.
14. **Bonuses.** Tease extra value, optionally with a scarcity/NDA wink.
15. **Core promise (numbered).** 2–3 big numbered outcomes: *"1 — OWN a system
    that replicates you at scale. 2 — UNLOCK the ability to create any asset."*
16. **Price anchoring + reveal.** Walk the ladder DOWN: *"We could've charged
    THOUSANDS… We're not charging $997. Not even $497… For just a single payment
    of $297!"* Then payment plan ("or 2 payments of $165"). Justify against the
    stacked value.
17. **Cost justification / reverse-flex.** *"We are literally LOSING money on this
    workshop. That's not marketing talk. That's actual MATH."* Add a spend callout
    (`$25K · Spent On That Week In Miami`).
18. **Hard deadline / urgency.** Tie to a real countdown: *"Once the countdown
    timer on this page hits zero, the door slams shut. We're not making exceptions
    for ANYONE."*
19. **Two paths close.** Side-by-side `Option #1: Keep doing what you're doing`
    (bulleted pain, framed as decline) vs `Option #2: You adapt` (bulleted gain).
    This forces a decision.
20. **Value recap + final CTA.** Re-stack the value, drop the price once more, and
    a long benefit-loaded button label: *"Attend the LIVE [Workshop]… PLUS Receive
    LIFETIME Access To All Recordings."*
21. **Sign-off.** First name, then `[Full Name] · [Title], [Company]`.
22. **P.S. stack (3 of them, each with a distinct job):**
    - **P.S.** — Re-frame the stakes as a future-self question. *"Where do you
      want to be 6 months from now?"*
    - **P.P.S.** — Reassure/remove risk. *"Remember, this comes with LIFETIME
      ACCESS to the recordings. So even if you can't make it live…"*
    - **P.P.P.S.** — The flex / proof-of-claim that closes the loop on the whole
      letter. The source page's killer move: *"This ENTIRE website you're reading?
      I coded the whole thing in Claude Code… in under 10 minutes."*

## 4. Reusable persuasion devices (steal these)

- **Name your mechanism.** A capitalized proper noun ("Dual Flywheel System",
  "Intelligence Stacking") makes the method feel ownable and new.
- **Specificity = proof.** Exact numbers, real place names, named people, dollar
  figures. Vague claims die; "$300M and $400M consumer brand portfolios at
  Boehringer Ingelheim" lives.
- **The price ladder.** Anchor high, strike it out verbally ($997 → $497 → $297),
  then offer a payment plan so the "real" decision feels even smaller.
- **The reverse-flex.** "We're losing money on this" reframes price as generosity.
- **The two-paths close.** Status-quo decline vs. transformation — make standing
  still feel like the risky choice.
- **The multi-P.S. stack.** Skimmers read the headline, the price, and the P.S.
  Make the P.S. carry the stakes, the risk-reversal, and the proof.
- **Soft-pressure framing.** "I'm not going to pressure you into a decision… but I
  do need you to understand what's REALLY at stake." Lowers the guard, then raises
  the stakes.
- **Coined in-group language.** "CLAUDEPILLED", "A-PLAYERS", "BUILDERS." Gives the
  reader an identity to step into.
