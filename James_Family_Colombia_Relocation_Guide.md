# Moving to Colombia with Your Family — Step-by-Step Guide

**For:** James (US citizen), his wife, and his minor daughter — relocating to Colombia in **summer 2026**, with the daughter starting school in **September 2026**, to help run his aunt's rental-property business and live there for a couple of years.

**Status:** Research summary for decision-making. **Not legal advice** — §9 lists the professionals to engage. Costs/thresholds are approximate and reset each January with Colombia's minimum wage (SMMLV); verify before filing.

> **One-paragraph version:** Your route is the **Digital Nomad visa** — the fastest way to land this summer with your daughter in school by September. You apply online (~2–4 weeks), with your **wife and daughter as dependents (beneficiaries)** on your visa. It requires ~**$1,400/month of your own remote US income** and does **not** let you draw income from your aunt's Colombian company, but it gets the family here quickly. In **parallel**, pursue your **wife's Argentine passport** (through her Argentine father) → Colombia's **Mercosur visa**, which gives *her* an open work permit and the family permanent residency in ~2 years — the best long-term anchor.

## 1. Your Visa — the Digital Nomad Route

You've decided on the **Digital Nomad visa (Visa V – Nómada Digital)** — the fastest way to relocate the family this summer.

| | Digital Nomad visa (your choice) |
|---|---|
| Speed | **Fastest** — apply online, ~2–4 weeks |
| Income proof | ~**$1,400/mo** of *foreign/remote* income (yours), each of the last 3 months |
| Dependents | **Wife + daughter included as beneficiaries** |
| Validity | **Up to 2 years**, renewable |
| Limits | ❌ Can't earn from your aunt's Colombian company · ❌ Doesn't count toward permanent residency |

**Two things to keep in mind:**
- It requires **your own remote/US-source income** (employer or clients). You **cannot** be paid by your aunt's Colombian company on this visa.
- It **doesn't build toward residency**, so for the long term, pursue your **wife's Argentine passport → Mercosur visa** in parallel (§6) — that's the family's path to permanent residency and open work rights.

### Recommended plan
1. **This summer:** You apply for the **Digital Nomad visa**, with **wife + daughter as beneficiaries**.
2. **In parallel:** Start your **wife's Argentine citizenship** (through her father) → when her passport comes, she gets the **Mercosur visa** (open work + residency in ~2 years), and the family can move onto that durable footing.

## 2. Step-by-Step — Landing This Summer (Digital Nomad route)

### Phase A — Before you leave the US (do now)
1. **Engage the team** (§9): a Colombian immigration lawyer, and (for the long-term track) an Argentine immigration lawyer/consulate for your wife.
2. **Apostille US documents** — your **marriage certificate** and your **daughter's birth certificate** (proving parentage), her **school transcripts**, and an **FBI background check**. Apostille via the issuing US state's Secretary of State (vital records) or the US Dept of State (FBI check). Get **official Spanish translations**.
3. **Gather your income proof** — 3 months of bank statements (**each** month ≥ ~$1,400), a **foreign-employer/client letter**, and buy **health insurance valid in Colombia**.
4. **Contact target schools NOW** (see §5 — Calendario B seats fill months ahead).

### Phase B — Apply for your Digital Nomad visa
5. **File online** at the Cancillería **SITAC** portal — from inside or outside Colombia (if inside, on your tourist stamp; don't let it lapse). Government fees ≈ **$54 study + ~$177 issuance**. Processing ~2–4 weeks.
6. **Register for your cédula de extranjería** at Migración Colombia **within 15 days** of visa issuance.

### Phase C — Bring your wife and daughter
7. **With your visa (or alongside it),** apply for their **beneficiary visas.** Documents: your apostilled **marriage certificate** (wife) and the apostilled **birth certificate** (daughter), plus a letter assuming their support. Their visas **mirror yours** and expire with yours.
8. **Cédulas** for them too (the daughter gets one if over 7), within 15 days.

> ⚠️ **Beneficiaries can study but not work** — your wife **cannot legally work** on a beneficiary visa (limited to "homemaker or student"); your **daughter CAN attend school**. For your wife to work, she'll want her **own Argentine/Mercosur visa** (§6).

## 3. If Your Remote Income Doesn't Qualify

The Digital Nomad visa needs ~**$1,400/mo of *foreign/remote* income** for **each** of the last 3 months. If you can't show that, the realistic fallbacks are: (a) **enter as a tourist** (90 days, extendable to 180/year) while your **wife's Argentine/Mercosur visa** comes through, then ride as her dependent; or (b) revisit a company-ownership (Socio) visa, which requires ~$44K–$51K of documented paid-in capital in your name. Discuss with the immigration lawyer before relying on the tourist bridge.

## 5. School for Your Daughter (the September deadline)

**Target "Calendario B" international/bilingual schools** — they run **August–June**, so September is their start of year. (Public and many private schools use "Calendario A," Feb–Nov, which has **no** September entry point.)

**Steps:**
1. **Contact target Calendario-B schools immediately.** Seats for an August/September start are often filled months ahead — a mid-2026 arrival is tight, so call now about mid-year availability.
2. **Prepare documents (apostille + Spanish translation in the US):**
   - Daughter's **birth certificate** (apostilled + translated)
   - **Prior school records/transcripts** (apostilled + translated)
   - **Passport**, **vaccination card**, and (when issued) her **visa/cédula**
3. **Enrollment vs. visa timing:** Public schools must enroll a minor **regardless of immigration status** (they assign a SIMAT/NES code). **Private/international schools set their own rules** — many will admit a child **while the visa is in process** (often on the tourist entry), but this is the **school's discretion** — confirm with each admissions office.
4. **Convalidación (grade validation):** Foreign studies are formally recognized by the Ministry of Education (**free, ~15 business days**). Many private schools admit on transcripts + their own placement exam and handle convalidación later (it mainly matters for the final high-school diploma).

## 6. The Argentine Passport Track (parallel, longer-term upgrade)

Why bother: an **Argentine passport** unlocks Colombia's **Mercosur visa** — the easiest of all: **no job, no investment, no income threshold**, just nationality + a clean background check, **open work permit**, and **permanent residency in just 2 years** (vs. 5 on other visas).

**Your daughter — via her Argentine father (faster):**
- She's entitled to Argentine citizenship by descent. Done by **consular registration of the child of an Argentine** (administrative, not court).
- **Timeline ~3–9 months**, mostly waiting for a US consulate appointment.
- Gives *her* independent Mercosur status. **A minor can't sponsor you for a *work* visa**, but Colombia's **TP-15 dependent route** lets the non-Mercosur family (you, your wife) get **dependent residence anchored to her** — you can live and she can study on it, though it **doesn't grant work rights** (so a working parent still needs their own income visa). Useful as a backup family anchor.

**You — via your Argentine mother:**
- Also entitled by descent (the "opción" mechanism, Ley 346). Good news: as an adult you can usually do this **administratively at an Argentine consulate** — it is **not** a mandatory federal-court case (that's only for naturalization or contested in-country files).
- **Two timelines:**
  - **Via a US consulate:** ~**6–12 months** for a clean case, mostly gated by appointment backlogs + the DNI being shipped from Buenos Aires (~3 months).
  - **🏃 Fastest — do it inside Argentina via RENAPER:** since you plan to travel to Argentina anyway, you can declare the option at the Registro Civil, get your **DNI (~1 month)**, then an **express passport (issued in hours/same-day)**. This is the quickest route to a passport in hand.
- ⚠️ Argentina changed its citizenship procedures in **2025 (Decreto 366/2025)** — confirm the current venue/process and fees with the consulate or an Argentine lawyer.
- **Dual citizenship is fine** — you keep your US citizenship (just enter/exit Argentina on the Argentine passport).
- **Confirm your mother is "native-born" Argentine** — required to pass citizenship down; and that her name matches exactly across your US birth certificate and her Argentine documents (name mismatches are the #1 cause of delay).

**Sequencing:** Move this summer on the Socio (or Digital Nomad) visa. Pursue both passports in parallel. When yours comes through, **switch to the Mercosur visa** for the easiest, fastest road to permanent residency.

---

## 7. How This Interacts With Taxes (brief)

- Spending **>183 days/year** in Colombia makes you a **Colombian tax resident** (worldwide income, up to 39%). Living there to run the business means you'll likely cross this — plan with a **US cross-border CPA** (there's **no US–Colombia tax treaty**, so coordination matters).
- Income your aunt's company pays you is **Colombian-source** and taxed in Colombia regardless.
- You still file **US taxes** (and likely **FBAR** once your Colombian accounts top $10K).

## 10. Key Risks & Reality Checks

- **School seats for September are the tightest deadline** — contact Calendario-B schools immediately and get documents apostilled now.
- **Your aunt's new SAS can't sponsor an employee work visa** (no banking history) — use the **Socio** (ownership) route instead.
- **Your wife can't work** on a dependent visa — plan around that (her own visa or her own Argentine passport if eligible).
- **Your Argentine passport is the slower piece** (~6–12 months via consulate, though faster if you do it in-country in Argentina via RENAPER) — treat it as an upgrade, not necessarily your summer plan, and confirm the post-2025 process.
- **Living there >183 days/year makes you a Colombian tax resident** — loop in a US cross-border CPA early.
- **Confirm everything year-specific** (SMMLV-based capital threshold, fees) with the lawyer before filing — they reset each January.
