Dear [Attorney Name],

Thank you for everything you've been handling on my property purchase — you know the transaction well, so I'll keep this focused on one thing I want us to get right: making sure I end up with the **best visa** to **live in Colombia, run the rental business, and travel freely back and forth to the U.S.**, on a path to permanent residency.

**My concern about the retirement visa.** I understand a **Pensionado (retirement) visa** has been suggested. I've since researched the rules and I'm concerned it isn't the right fit, because: (1) it requires a **lifetime pension** of roughly 3× the minimum wage, and income from investments, rentals, or savings doesn't qualify; (2) it **does not permit actively running a business** — which is exactly what I intend to do with the rental houses; and (3) it's temporary and takes **five years** to reach residency. I'd much rather pursue an **investment-based residency** that matches what I'm actually doing here.

**The obstacle.** As you know, my purchase is being paid over time. I am about to make my next installment of **US$150,000**, after which I will owe approximately **US$400,000** on the balance. Under the current *promesa de compraventa*, **title doesn't transfer to me until that balance is paid.** My understanding is that the **investor/resident visa requires the *Certificado de Tradición y Libertad* in my name**, which I won't have for a couple of years under the current arrangement. So I'd like your help solving that. I see two routes:

**Plan A (preferred) — Help us get the seller to transfer title now.**
Could you approach the seller about **transferring title to me now, with a registered mortgage (*hipoteca*) in his favor securing the remaining ~US$400,000 balance**? Since he is a Colombian resident, this is a standard seller-financing structure that **fully secures him** (he can foreclose if I default) while giving me **title in my name**. With that, I can complete my foreign-investment registration and qualify for the **Visa R (Resident) by investment** right away, rather than waiting years. I'd appreciate your guidance on proposing this to him and drafting the *escritura* and *hipoteca* accordingly.

**Plan B (fallback) — If the seller won't transfer title now, let's set up a Colombian company.**
If he won't agree to deed the property now, then I'd like to **form a Colombian SAS to hold the investment** and pursue the **Visa M – Socio o Propietario (business-owner visa)**, which is keyed to my **registered investment in the company rather than to holding real-estate title** — so I could qualify before the property is fully paid. This route also fits my plan to **operate the rental business**.

**A few things to confirm along the way.** Please make sure each payment I send continues to be **channeled through the bank with the *declaración de cambio* registering it as foreign investment**, so my registration and repatriation rights build up correctly. And please confirm the **title is clean (*estudio de títulos*)** and that the funds I've already paid are **protected** (via the mortgage-on-title structure or a *fiducia*/escrow) given I don't yet hold title.

**What I'd like from you.** Your recommendation between Plan A and Plan B, the **steps and documents** needed (and which require apostille/translation), the **timeline**, and your **fees/scope**. I'm glad to jump on a call with you and the seller's side whenever useful, and I'm also coordinating with a U.S. cross-border CPA.

Thank you again — I'm grateful for your help getting this right.
